# Topic Selection

Theme: **VIS + AI** (analysing how a robot policy's action chunks are *executed* under inference latency).

## 1. Literature survey: what the field says and where the pain is

| Work | What it says about chunk execution / latency | How it is evaluated / shown today |
|---|---|---|
| ACT (Zhao et al., 2023, arXiv 2304.13705) | Chunk k=100; *temporal ensembling* queries every step and averages overlapping predictions → smooth but less reactive | Success-rate tables |
| Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023, arXiv 2303.04137) | Prediction horizon 16, action (execution) horizon 8; short horizon → jittery mode switching, long → not reactive | One ablation line plot (success vs. action horizon) |
| π0 (Black et al., 2024, arXiv 2410.24164) | Flow-matching chunks H=50, partially executed open-loop | Success bars |
| FAST (Pertsch et al., 2025, arXiv 2501.09747) | Tokenised chunks; decode latency becomes the bottleneck | Tables |
| OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025, arXiv 2502.19645) | Parallel decoding + chunking for throughput | Tables |
| SmolVLA (Shukor et al., 2025, arXiv 2506.01844) | **Asynchronous inference**: robot keeps executing the queue while the server predicts the next chunk | Queue-size-over-time debug plot |
| Bidirectional Decoding (Liu et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2408.17355) | Names the **consistency vs. reactivity** dilemma of chunking | Success tables |
| Real-Time Chunking, RTC (Black et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2506.07339) | Treat the next chunk as **inpainting**: freeze the actions that will be executed during the inference delay *d* | Success vs. delay / execution-horizon line plots |
| Training-time RTC (Black et al., 2025, arXiv 2512.05964) | Simulated delay during training | Line plots |
| VLASH (Tang et al., 2025, arXiv 2512.01031) | Stale observation ↔ action misalignment under async inference | Tables |
| SEAM (Zhan et al., 2026, arXiv 2607.04609) | Defines *boundary jerk*, *chunk-transition discontinuity*; "intention conflict" vs "position gap" at boundaries | Tables + jerk curves |
| Chunk-Boundary Artifact (Wang, 2026, arXiv 2603.11642) | Failed episodes show larger boundary artifacts | Jerk time-courses with dashed lines at replans |

**Recurring pain points**

1. *Stale actions*: under async inference the action executed at time τ was planned from an observation of time τ − d. Nobody shows *where in an episode* staleness matters.
2. *Seams*: discontinuities where chunk *k+1* takes over from chunk *k* (position gaps, mode/intention switches).
3. *Choosing (strategy, latency, execution horizon)* is done by hand from aggregate success curves; an aggregate curve hides *which episodes* break and *why*.
4. Existing "visualisations" are developer debug plots (queue-size plots, matplotlib jerk curves). A search of IEEE VIS / EuroVis / CHI found RL visual analytics (DQNViz, DRLViz, PolicyExplainer) but **no visual-analytics work on action-chunk execution or asynchronous inference**.

## 2. Three candidate topics

### A. LagLens — paired counterfactual replay of chunk execution schedules
- **Analysis problem**: For a *fixed* policy and a *fixed* set of initial states, how do execution strategy (sync / naive async / inpainting async), latency *d* and execution horizon *h* change *what is executed*, *where seams occur*, and *which episodes flip from success to failure*?
- **Data**: generated by us. Pretrained `lerobot/diffusion_pusht` run in the PushT simulator on CPU; every predicted chunk (16 future actions) and every executed action is logged; the same 32 initial states (paired seeds) are replayed under ~30 schedules. Feasibility verified: ~2–4 s per batched inference on 12 CPU cores.
- **Core idioms**: schedule matrix (strategy × d × h) as a navigator; paired-seed "flip" matrix; episode workspace replay with the *fan* of predicted chunks and executed path; chunk timeline (Gantt of observation → available → executed, staleness band, seam ticks).
- **Novelty**: no VIS tool for chunk execution; *paired counterfactual* comparison (same initial state, different schedule) and *chunk-provenance* encoding (every executed action linked back to the observation it was planned from) cannot be built in Tableau/Excel.
- **Risk**: compute time (mitigated by batching; overnight runs), simulator coordinate mapping.

### B. ModeBraid — mode switching inside diffusion-policy chunks
- **Analysis problem**: at each re-plan, the diffusion policy can choose a different behaviour mode; when do consecutive chunks disagree (SEAM's "intention conflict") and does that predict failure?
- **Data**: generated; *K* samples per observation to estimate the mode distribution (K× more compute).
- **Core idioms**: braided ribbons of sampled chunks, clustering of modes over time.
- **Novelty**: high, but the multi-sample data is ~10× costlier on CPU, and the analysis needs a mode-clustering step whose validity is hard to justify in the time available.
- **Risk**: high (compute, clustering validity).

### C. Latency Atlas — meta-analysis of published async-inference results
- **Analysis problem**: compare reported success vs. delay / horizon across RTC, BID, SmolVLA, VLASH, SEAM.
- **Data**: numbers transcribed from paper tables/figures (≈100–200 rows), heterogeneous benchmarks.
- **Core idioms**: line charts / dot plots across papers.
- **Novelty**: low — the result is close to a Tableau dashboard; numbers are not comparable across benchmarks.
- **Risk**: low technically, high on the "generic interface" penalty.

## 3. Scoring (1 = poor, 5 = excellent)

| Criterion (weight) | A. LagLens | B. ModeBraid | C. Latency Atlas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Novelty (×2) | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| Interactivity potential (×1.5) | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| Effectiveness for a concrete analysis task (×1.5) | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| Data feasibility on our CPU-only machine (×1) | 4 | 2 | 4 |
| Completable by deadlines (×1) | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| **Weighted total (max 35)** | **33.0** | **26.0** | **19.5** |

## 4. Decision

**A. LagLens**. It directly targets the pain points 1–3 above, its data is produced by us with a real pretrained policy (not a generic open dataset), and its core interaction, *switch the schedule and see the same initial states replayed*, is specific to this problem. The most valuable idea from B (detecting intention conflict at seams) is kept as a seam metric inside A: the jump between the outgoing chunk's planned action and the incoming chunk's first executed action.
