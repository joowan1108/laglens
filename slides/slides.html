LagLens: Seeing What a Robot Actually Executes When Its Policy Thinks Too Slowly

A visual analytics tool for action-chunk execution under inference latency (sync vs. asynchronous inference, open-loop execution horizon)

Modern robot policies (Diffusion Policy, ACT, π0, SmolVLA) predict a chunk of future actions and execute part of it open-loop. Inference takes time d, so the robot either waits (sync) or keeps executing the old chunk and switches when the new one arrives (async).
  • Stale actions: under async inference an action executed at time τ was planned from the observation at τ−d (VLASH, 2025).
  • Discontinuities: where chunk k+1 replaces chunk k, the plans can disagree → jumps, mode switches (BID 2025; SEAM 2026).
  • Tuning by hand: strategy, latency budget and execution horizon are chosen from aggregate success-vs-delay curves (RTC 2025; LeRobot async docs).
  • Gap: today's views are debug plots and tables. We found no visual analytics work on chunk execution or async inference (IEEE VIS / EuroVis / CHI search).
Our idea: paired counterfactual replay. We run one pretrained policy from the same 50 initial states under 46 execution schedules and log every predicted chunk. Analysts can then ask: “Which episodes flip from success to failure when I go async with 400 ms latency, and is it the stale actions or the discontinuities that cause it?”

1 · What — Data Abstraction

Self-generated rollout logs of a pretrained diffusion policy

Dataset type: multidimensional table of episodes, each linked to two temporal tables and to a spatial field (512 × 512 px workspace).
  • Step table (598,117 items) · keys: schedule, seed, t (ordinal, 10 Hz) · values: agent x, y; block x, y (interval, px); block θ (cyclic, rad); action x, y (interval, px); chunk id (ordinal); index j 0–15 (ordinal); age (ratio, steps); held (binary); coverage (ratio 0–1).
  • Chunk table (119,373 items) · keys: schedule, seed, chunk id · values: t_obs, t_avail (ordinal time), executed window, 16 predicted (x, y) actions.
  • Size: 215 KB episode CSV + 112 MB step/chunk JSON (lazy-loaded per schedule).
Keys vs. values: keys = (strategy, latency, horizon, replicate) → schedule; × seed → episode; × t → step; × chunk id → chunk. Everything else is a value.
Novel data: no public dataset logs per-inference chunks (survey, Oct 2026). Generated by us; scripts in scripts/. 1 step = 100 ms.
Model: Chi et al., Diffusion Policy (2023), checkpoint by Hugging Face LeRobot · PushT env: gym-pusht2 / 7

3 · Why — Task Abstraction (action + target, Munzner)

Four tasks, from schedules down to a single discontinuity

#Action + targetConcrete question (Mara)WhyExpected outcome
T1Compare → trends of success / boundary discontinuity / staleness across schedules “How fast does success fall with latency for each strategy, and which horizon is most robust?” Choose a candidate schedule for the latency the lab actually has. A shortlist of robust schedules and a latency budget per strategy.
T2Locate → outliers: initial states whose outcome flips between a reference and a candidate schedule “Going from sync d=0 to naive async d=4, which seeds start failing, and which are rescued?” Aggregate rates hide which situations break; paired seeds isolate the schedule's effect. A small set of fragile seeds to inspect (and to add to a regression test set).
T3Identify → features in time & space: where stale actions, discontinuities and idling occur in a flipped episode “At which step does the candidate diverge from the reference, and was the executed action stale, a discontinuity, or a wait?” Diagnose the mechanism of failure, not just its rate. A causal hypothesis, e.g. “a 30 px discontinuity right at block contact makes the push slip.”
T4Discover → correlation between boundary discontinuities (plan inconsistency) and task outcome (coverage) over paired episodes “When a seed moves from R to C, do larger discontinuities go with lower coverage? Does inpainting shrink the discontinuities?” Recent work claims failed episodes show larger chunk-boundary artifacts (Wang 2026); check it on our policy. Evidence for or against a strategy (direction and size of shift per seed).
Arjun mainly needs T1 (latency budget) and T2 (is the drop driven by a few seeds?). Mara chains T1 → T2 → T3 → T4 in one session: overview first, then drill down to a single chunk hand-over (details on demand).
Task vocabulary: Munzner, Visualization Analysis & Design (2014), Ch. 34 / 7

Why not a dashboard: views are driven by a reference/candidate pair and by chunk provenance (each executed action is linked to the observation and the chunk it came from). Less is more: the first draft had 5 views and ~10 hues; following the rules of parsimony and consistency [Wang Baldonado 00] we merged the two seed-level views, gave every hue one meaning, and cut ten technical controls to one question bar named after who pays each cost.
