LagLens: What Does a Robot Do While Its Policy Is Still Thinking?

A visual analytics tool for robot policies that plan in chunks and need time to think

How such a robot works
  1. Plan. A robot policy (a neural network) looks at the camera once and plans its next 16 moves: an action chunk.
  2. Act. The robot performs the first h moves of the chunk without looking again, then needs a new chunk.
  3. Think. Computing a chunk takes time d (inference latency, here 0–600 ms). What should the robot do meanwhile? (right)
    • Sync: wait. Safe, but the robot stands idle.
    • Async: keep moving on the old chunk. No waiting, but the scene changes while the policy thinks, so the new chunk is made for a scene that no longer exists (an outdated plan) and may not continue the current motion: the robot jumps when it switches (a chunk-boundary discontinuity). Naive async just swaps the chunks; inpaint async computes the new chunk so that it continues the old one.
The problem. Given their latency d, engineers pick a strategy and h by comparing one success-rate number per combination, e.g. “naive async, d = 4: {{SR_NAIVE_D4_H8}} vs. sync, d = 0: {{SR_SYNC_D0_H8}}”. That number cannot show which start states failed, when, or why (idling, outdated plans or jumps). We found no visualization tool for this.
Our idea: same start, different schedule. We run one pretrained policy from the same {{N_SEEDS}} start states under {{N_SCHED}} schedules (strategy × d × h) and record every planned chunk. Any two schedules can then be compared start by start and replayed move by move.

1 · What — Data Abstraction

Our own recorded runs of a pretrained robot policy

Task (PushT, simulated, top view): a round pusher (the agent) must push a T-shaped block onto a fixed T-shaped goal. Policy: a pretrained Diffusion Policy (lerobot/diffusion_pusht) that turns the last 2 camera frames and pusher positions into a chunk of 16 moves (target positions of the pusher). It builds every chunk from random noise, so running the same schedule again can change an outcome; one noise replicate measures how often. Same {{N_SEEDS}} start states × ({{N_SCHED}} schedules + 1 noise replicate) = {{N_EP}} episodes (recorded runs, also called rollouts); every planned chunk and every executed move is logged.

Ordering direction: ↑ higher is better · ↓ lower is better · no arrow = no preferred direction.
Dataset type: multidimensional table of episodes, each linked to two temporal tables and to a spatial field (512 × 512 px workspace).
Data types: items (episodes, steps, chunks) with attributes; positions (x, y of pusher, block and planned moves).
  • Step table ({{N_STEPS}} items) · keys: schedule, seed, t (ordinal, 10 Hz) · values: agent x, y; block x, y (interval, px); block θ (cyclic, rad); action x, y (interval, px); chunk id (ordinal); index j 0–15 (ordinal); age (ratio, steps since its camera picture); held (binary, 1 = idle step); coverage (ratio 0–1, block–goal overlap).
  • Chunk table ({{N_CHUNKS}} items) · keys: schedule, seed, chunk id · values: t_obs (time of the camera picture), t_avail (time the chunk was ready), executed window, 16 predicted (x, y) actions.
  • Size: {{CSV_KB}} KB episode CSV + {{DETAIL_MB}} MB step/chunk JSON (lazy-loaded per schedule).
Keys vs. values: keys = (strategy, latency, horizon, replicate) → schedule; × seed → episode; × t → step; × chunk id → chunk. Everything else is a value.
Novel data: no public dataset logs per-inference chunks (survey, Oct 2026). Generated by us; scripts in scripts/. 1 step = 100 ms.
Model: Chi et al., Diffusion Policy (2023), checkpoint by Hugging Face LeRobot · PushT env: gym-pusht2 / 7

3 · Why — Task Abstraction (action + target)

Four tasks, from all schedules down to a single chunk switch

#Action + targetConcrete question (Mara)WhyExpected outcome
T1Compare → trends of success and of each strategy's cost (idle time, outdated plans, jumps) across schedules “How fast does success fall with latency for each strategy, and which horizon is most robust?” Choose a candidate schedule for the latency the lab actually has. A shortlist of robust schedules and a latency budget per strategy.
T2Locate → outliers: initial states whose outcome flips between a baseline and a candidate schedule “Going from sync d=0 to naive async d=4, which seeds start failing, and which are rescued?” Aggregate rates hide which situations break; paired seeds isolate the schedule's effect. A small set of fragile seeds to inspect (and to add to a regression test set).
T3Identify → features in time & space: where idling, outdated plans and jumps occur in a flipped episode “At which step does the candidate diverge from the baseline, and did the robot wait, act on an outdated plan, or jump?” Diagnose the mechanism of failure, not just its rate. A causal hypothesis, e.g. “a 30 px discontinuity right at block contact makes the push slip.”
T4Discover → correlation between jumps at chunk switches (boundary discontinuities) and task outcome (coverage) over paired episodes “When a seed moves from the baseline to the candidate, do larger discontinuities go with lower coverage? Does inpainting shrink the discontinuities?” If jumps are what breaks episodes, a strategy that shrinks them should rescue seeds; check it on our policy. Evidence for or against a strategy (direction and size of shift per seed).
Arjun mainly needs T1 (latency budget) and T2 (is the drop driven by a few seeds?). Mara chains T1 → T2 → T3 → T4 in one session: overview first, then drill down to a single chunk switch (details on demand).
Actions: compare · locate · identify · discover — targets: trends · outliers · features · correlation; from overview (T1) to a single chunk switch (T3)4 / 7
4 · How — Vis Idiom Design (4 linked views, overview → filter → details)

Each view answers one task with the most effective idiom

ViewTaskIdiom & why (VIS literature)Interaction
A · Latency ResponseT1
“How fast does each strategy get worse as the policy gets slower?”
Line chart small multiples: one panel per horizon h, x = latency d on its true scale, y = metric, strategies superposed (hue + shape). Position on a common scale beats the luminance of a heatmap [Cleveland & McGill 84; Heer & Bostock 10]; superposition for direct sync-vs-async comparison, juxtaposition across h [Gleicher 11]. 95 % interval lines instead of bars [Correll & Gleicher 14]; hover = paired difference to the baseline.		Click mark = candidate, shift-click = baseline; one question bar: Success · Idle (cost of sync) · Outdated (cost of async) · Discontinuity (cost of naive async), drives A and B; recomputes on seeds selected in B.
B · Paired Seed TableT2, T4
“Which start states flip between baseline and candidate, and is that more than chance?”
Composite Table-Lens view [Rao & Card 94; Javed & Elmqvist 12]: one row per seed shared by (left) an outcome matrix — filled = success, baseline column black, candidate column orange — and (right) dumbbells baseline → candidate of the attribute that belongs to A's question. Rows are grouped by baseline→candidate change (spatial region), so flips break their band's pattern; a re-run column shows chance flips (11/50).		No controls of its own. Click column header = candidate; click cell = open episode; brush dumbbells or click a band → cross-filter A [Weaver 10].
C · Workspace ReplayT3
“Watch both runs side by side: what was planned, what was done, where it jumped.”
Juxtaposed baseline | candidate maps at identical scale (true geometry). Overlays: current chunk, executed (solid) and thrown away (dashed); next chunk being computed (dotted); latest discontinuity (magenta); what the policy saw (dotted outline of block & pusher in the chunk's camera picture, arrow to the robot now: the gap = how outdated the plan is).		Play / scrub / ← → (navigate t); hover a planned point → when planned / executed; click → jump.
D · Execution TimelineT3
“When was the policy thinking, which plan did each move come from, where were the jumps?”
Gantt lanes on a shared linear time axis [Plaisant 96; Brehmer 17]: per run a think track (inference) and an act track (chunk spans, hatched = idle), a motion track (movement per step, jumps as magenta bars on the same scale), coverage lines below. Sync shows think/act alternating, async overlapping. The chunk at the cursor is traced picture ○ → think → act, with a 16-cell strip of its planned moves: skipped while thinking · executed · discarded.		Brush a time window → highlighted path in view C; hover chunk → its plan in view C; click → cursor.
Why this is not a generic dashboard: C and D trace every executed move back to the plan (chunk) and the camera frame it came from, which no chart tool offers; A and B compare two schedules start state by start state against a chance baseline (re-run), not just averages. Less is more: the first draft had 5 views and ~10 hues; following parsimony and consistency [Wang Baldonado 00] we merged two views, gave every hue one meaning and cut ten controls to one question bar.
Shneiderman's mantra (1996): overview first (A), zoom & filter (B), details on demand (C, D) · interactions after Heer & Shneiderman (2012)5 / 7
